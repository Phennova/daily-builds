How it works

A CD is one spiral. About 20,000 turns, 1.6 micrometres apart, read at a constant linear speed of about 1.3 m/s. Every 1/7350 s of audio (one 24-byte frame) occupies the next ~0.18 mm of track. Burn a frame with byte patterns that leave long marks and that stretch of dye goes dark; pick patterns with short marks and it stays light. Choose every frame right and the whole disc shows a picture. Real tools do this today (CDImage, DiscImageStudio).

Why it is fragile. To know where frame number 20,000,000 lands, you multiply by the velocity and wrap around the spiral twenty thousand times. Get the velocity wrong by one part in a hundred thousand and the outer rings land a whole radian off: the picture twists into a swirl. That is why such images only work on the disc model they were calibrated for.

Your controls. v linear velocity, p track pitch, r0 start radius, φ0 start angle. Drag a jog strip (or use - / +) to change a value; the step buttons choose how fine a step is. Coarse first, then finer. Calibration spokes near the hub bow when the geometry is off: make them straight.

The lag scope. Adjacent turns of the spiral are only 1.6 um apart, so they carry almost the same picture. The stream therefore echoes itself after exactly one revolution. Autocorrelation finds that echo: the peak lag is the number of frames per turn at that point. Two readings at different positions pin down the geometry (given a start radius); your eyes do the last few digits.

Listen. Plays the actual frames as audio. Dark and light frames are two sample patterns, so you hear the picture as a rasp that flutters once per revolution.

Keyboard. Up/Down pick a parameter, Left/Right jog it, [ and ] change the step, Enter submits the flag.

Model notes: frames are dark/light at the frame level; real burns also go through CIRC interleaving and EFM modulation, which real tools pre-compensate. This game does not produce burnable files.

Make a dead drop

Type a short message. It gets burned onto a disc with random geometry, and you get a link. Whoever opens it has to calibrate the disc to read it. (The link scrambles the text with a seed: a toy, not encryption.)
