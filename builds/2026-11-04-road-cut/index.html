    ROAD CUT

    Read the rocks. Rewind 500 million years.

    Every cliff on this road trip was made by actually simulating deep time: seas laying down sediment, the crust tilting, folding and faulting, magma forcing its way up, erosion planing it all flat. Put the events in order, oldest first, using the clues a field geologist uses. Get it right and the cliff rebuilds itself before your eyes.

    Field guide

    1. Superposition. Layers pile up from the bottom: lower layers are older.
    2. Original horizontality. Sediment settles flat. Tilted or folded layers were bent later, so the tilting or folding is younger than every layer it bends. Flatter layers on top came after.
    3. Cross-cutting. Whatever cuts through rock is younger than that rock: dikes, granite plutons and faults. A dike that crosses a fault without being broken came after the fault.
    4. Unconformities. A heavy wavy line is an old erosion surface: the land rose, was worn down, then sank and was buried again. Everything it cuts is older than the erosion; everything sitting on it is younger. A fault that stops at the line happened before the erosion.
    5. When the rocks can't tell. Some pairs have no contact that proves their order (say, a fault and a tilt). Any order the evidence allows is accepted.

    Rock key
