BARE EARTH

Find a lost Maya city under the jungle using nothing but laser pulses.

An aircraft has swept 800 m of rainforest with lidar. Millions of pulses bounced off leaves; a few slipped through to the ground. Filter them, light the terrain from any sun you like, slice through the point cloud, and report what humans built here. Inspired by a November 2026 find of an unmapped Maya site in public NASA G-LiHT lidar. Everything below is simulated and the sites are fictional.

How lidar archaeology works

  1. Pulses. Each 2 m cell got about five laser pulses. Most hit the canopy (green) or the understory; in dense forest only a few percent reach the ground (brown).
  2. Ground filter. The filter keeps the lowest return in each cell, then runs a progressively larger morphological opening (Zhang et al. 2003). Window = the widest blob it can strip off: too small and patches with no ground returns stay in as fake mounds. Slope = how steep real ground may be: too loose keeps understory junk, too tight and it treats a pyramid like a tree and cuts its top off.
  3. Views. Hillshade lights the bare earth from a sun you drag; features that run along the light vanish. 3-sun RGB puts a sun from the north, south-east and south-west in the red, green and blue channels, so nothing hides from all three. Relief (local relief model) subtracts the smoothed terrain so big hills disappear and metre-high mounds pop. Sky-view shows how much sky each point sees: no blind direction. Coverage shows where the filter had real ground points (bright) and where it guessed (red). Canopy is what a satellite sees.
  4. Profile. Switch to Slice and drag a line across a feature. You see the raw points along it. A real structure has brown ground points on it. A "mound" sitting on green points 3 m up is the filter fooling you.
  5. Report. Choose a type and tap the map to pin it (tap a pin to remove it). Types: Pyramid (tall, stepped), Platform (flat-topped, 1-3 m, straight edges), Mound (house mound, under ~1.3 m), Causeway (raised, dead straight road), Reservoir (flat-bottomed hollow with a berm). Then submit: the heritage institute checks your report against the ground truth.

Scoring: +100 per real feature, +50 if the type is right, -75 per false positive. Each strip has a quota and a false-positive limit. Not archaeology: karst cone hills (round, lumpy), sinkholes (funnel-shaped), tree-throws (pit and mound from a fallen tree), modern logging tracks (sunken, curving).

Controls: drag to pan, pinch or wheel to zoom. Keys: 1-8 views, P pin, S slice, arrow keys pan, +/- zoom, M mute.
