BLADE RATE

passive sonar · five watches · one quiet boat

You are the sonar supervisor of a fast-attack submarine on a barrier patrol. You cannot ping. You can only listen. Steer your beam onto a contact, read its machinery tones on the LOFAR, its propeller rhythm on the DEMON, check the intelligence book, and report what it is and how fast it is going.

Headphones recommended. Every display is a real FFT of the sound being simulated.

How to read the stack

BROADBAND (top): bearing across (000 = our bow, centre), time flowing down. Ships draw bright streaks. The hatched edges are our baffles (we are deaf astern). Tap or drag to steer the listening beam; ◀ ▶ or arrow keys fine-tune it by 1°.

LOFAR: frequency across (Hz), time down; the trace on top is the averaged spectrum. Rotating machinery makes steady vertical lines. A ship's electrical plant hums at 50 Hz or 60 Hz with harmonics at 2x, 3x: drag the harmonic cursor onto a line and its ticks show where the harmonics should be. Whales make lines that sweep and come and go.

DEMON (Detection of Envelope Modulation On Noise): the hiss of a cavitating propeller pulses once per blade. DEMON takes the envelope of that hiss and shows its rhythm. Put the cursor on the shaft rate (the small line at far left): its ticks then mark 1x, 2x, 3x shaft; the tall blade-rate line sits on tick N, where N = number of blades. Shaft rate x 60 = RPM.

Speed = RPM / turns-per-knot (TPK, from the book). A quiet submarine does not cavitate: use its blade-rate line low on the LOFAR (zoom to 0-60 Hz) and divide by its blade count.

INT (integration) averages more spectra: faint lines appear, but the picture reacts more slowly.

REPORT classifies whatever is on the beam bearing. Points: class 100, speed within 2 kn +50 (within 4 kn +20). Calling a Red boat friendly costs 50. Reporting empty water costs 25.

Keys: arrows steer (shift = 5°), I integration, Z zoom, R report, B book, M mute.
