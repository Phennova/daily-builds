CALL THE BALL

Night or day, every Navy carrier landing ends the same way: about 18 seconds in "the groove", flying a single amber light on the deck while the Landing Signal Officer watches and grades every pass.

How to fly the ball

  • Power is your glideslope. More throttle = less sink. The engines take about a second to respond, so make small corrections and wait. Chasing the ball makes you porpoise.
  • Lineup. The landing area is angled 9° from the ship's heading, so as the ship steams ahead the centreline creeps to the right. Keep nudging right to stay on it.
  • Listen to Paddles (the LSO): "Power", "Right for lineup", "Easy with it". If you hear "Wave off!" go to full power immediately. Landing after a wave-off is a Cut.
  • Grades: OK (4 pts), Fair (3), Bolter (2.5), No Grade (2), Wave-off (1), Cut (0). The LSO writes comments like (LO)IC = a little low in close.
Touch / mouse: press anywhere and drag. Up/down = throttle (stays where you leave it). Left/right = bank (springs back).
Keyboard: W/S or ↑/↓ throttle, A/D or ←/→ bank, Space = full power, P pause, M mute.

Practice

Pick conditions. Practice passes are not saved to the cruise board.

You start in the groove, about 0.6 nm behind the ship, on speed. Fly the ball, keep the lineup.
